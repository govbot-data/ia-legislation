29 BACKGROUND. This bill amends or enacts provisions relating
30 to different types of business entities existing under the
31 jurisdiction of the secretary of state (secretary), and
32 specifically the secretary’s filing office. A business
33 entity is composed of one or more persons holding a legal
34 interest in a for-profit business entity, cooperative interest
35 in a cooperative association, or beneficial interest in a
LSB 5534XD (5) 91

1 nonprofit business entity, with the interest holders pooling
2 capital, and being shielded from personal liability from the
3 acts or omissions of the business entity. Specifically, the
4 bill provides for business entities formed in this state
5 (domestic) or formed outside this state and authorized to do or
6 transact business in this state (foreign) as provided in their
7 respective governing Code chapters. These business entities
8 include partnerships (Code chapter 486A), including limited
9 liability partnerships; limited partnerships (Code chapter
10 488); limited liability companies (Code chapter 489); business
11 corporations (Code chapter 490); traditional cooperative
12 associations (Code chapter 499); housing cooperatives (Code
13 chapter 499A); closed cooperatives (Code chapter 501);
14 cooperative associations (Code chapter 501A); and nonprofit
15 corporations (Code chapter 504). Generally, to be recognized
16 as a business entity, the entity must comply with a number of
17 requirements as set forth in its governing Code chapter. It
18 must file a number of specified documents or records relating
19 to the organization or management of the entity, including a
20 document or record establishing its existence (e.g., for a
21 domestic business corporation, articles of incorporation and
22 for a foreign business corporation, a foreign registration
23 statement). A business entity must maintain a registered agent
24 in the state who may accept service of process. The business
25 entity may also be subject to administrative dissolution
26 proceedings by the secretary under certain circumstances (e.g.,
27 a failure to pay taxes, a failure to deliver a biennial report,
28 or a failure to retain a registered agent).
29 BILL’S PROVISIONS —— GENERAL. The bill amends provisions
30 governing business entities in two ways: (1) by providing
31 for the removal of false information in a filing, and (2) by
32 providing for the administrative dissolution of the business
33 entity based on the responses to interrogatories served on
34 the business entity indicating the business entity’s wrongful
35 conduct.
